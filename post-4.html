The metric everyone tracks and nobody trusts

Clicks are easy to count. Attention is harder, slower, and often much closer to the thing we actually care about.

Clicks have a comforting property: they arrive as integers. They can be sorted, compared, and put on a weekly slide. But a click is not necessarily attention, and attention is not necessarily interest.

What the number misses

Attention has duration, quality, and context. Someone may pause with a piece of work because it helped them understand a problem. Or because the page was confusing. The number alone does not tell you which.

A metric is most helpful when it keeps a question alive, not when it pretends to end one.

The aim is not a perfect attention score. It is a better conversation about whether the work is genuinely being noticed, understood, and remembered.
